--- layout: example.html title: Vector Image Layer Rotation shortdesc: Effect of the VectorImageLayer rotateContent option on a rotated view. docs: >

Both maps share one view and show the same fictional apron. The left map is a plain VectorLayer - the accuracy reference. The right map is a VectorImageLayer, with a checkbox to toggle its rotateContent option.

A VectorImageLayer renders its vectors to an image and then copies that image into the layer canvas. By default the image is rendered north-up: on a rotated view its extent has fractional pixel dimensions, so the copy lands on a fractional offset and gets resampled - and the whole canvas is then rotated by the browser, resampling a second time. That double resampling is the blur on the right with the checkbox off, and it also rotates every symbol and label with the view, whatever their rotateWithView setting says. The readout next to the checkbox shows where the copy lands.

With rotateContent the image is rendered screen-aligned instead: the view rotation is applied to the geometry, symbols and labels are snapped to whole pixels at their final screen orientation, the settled copy to the canvas is lossless, and rotateWithView behaves like on a VectorLayer. When the view is settled, the right map is then at least as crisp as the reference on the left - in exchange, the image has to be re-rendered after every rotation change.

The two label kinds are sized differently on purpose. The apron name and the guidance-line designator keep a fixed screen size, so their size relative to the pavement changes as you zoom. The stand designators are sized in map units instead - the font size tracks the view resolution, so a designator keeps a fixed relationship to its stand box, and the labels are dropped once the view is zoomed too far out to place them.

Both maps load north-up, where the layers render nearly identically. Use the slider above, or rotate either map with Alt+Shift+Drag, and toggle the checkbox to compare - the difference persists at any angle except 0. Panning and zooming stay in sync as well, since both maps share one view.
